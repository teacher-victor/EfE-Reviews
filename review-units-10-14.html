Review: Units 10–14

Unit 10.1

Talking about jobs

Use a form of to be (am, is, are) with a or an and a job title to say what someone does. Use a before a job that starts with a consonant sound, and an before a job that starts with a vowel sound. Plural jobs do not use a or an.

I am a police officer. He is an engineer. She is a nurse. They are teachers. We are artists.

Choosing a or an

First soundArticleExample
Consonant soundaa teacher
Vowel soundanan engineer
Plural jobno articleteachers

Say the job title out loud to check the sound, not just the first letter — "an hour" starts with a vowel sound even though "hour" begins with the letter H.

  1. (I) I a taxi driver.
  2. (he) He an actor.
  3. (they) They firefighters.
  4. a job that starts with a vowel sound She is engineer.
  5. a job that starts with a consonant sound He is chef.
  6. (we) We doctors.
  7. asking about someone's job you a teacher?
  8. plural jobs need no article My parents accountants.

Reading practice

At the job fair, everyone talks about their work. Amara says, "I am a nurse at the city hospital." Next to her, a tall man smiles. "I'm Tomas. I am an electrician." Two students walk by with name tags. "We are volunteers today," they say. A woman in a red jacket introduces her husband. "This is my husband. He is a firefighter." Later, a group of teenagers stops at a table about computers. "Are you a programmer?" one asks the man at the table. "Yes," he says, "I am a programmer for a small company." By the end of the day, everyone knows a little more about the jobs at the fair.

Check your understanding

1. Amara is a nurse.

2. Tomas is a firefighter.

3. The two students are volunteers.

4. The woman's husband is a firefighter.

5. The man at the computer table says he is not a programmer.

Units 10.8, 10.11

Using "work in," "work on," and "work with"

Use work in for a place or building, like an office or a hospital. Use work on for a specific site or project, like a farm, a ship, or a film set. Use work with for people, animals, or things you work alongside.

I work in a hospital. I work on a farm. I work with animals. She works in an office downtown. He works with computers all day.

work in / on / with

PhraseUsed forExample
work ina building or placework in a school
work ona specific site or projectwork on a farm
work withpeople, animals, or thingswork with children

A farm, a ship, and a film set are places too, but we use "work on" for these specific kinds of locations, not "work in."

Units 11.1, 11.2

Telling the time

Use It's with a number to say the hour, or with o'clock for a full hour. Use half past for 30 minutes after the hour, and quarter past or quarter to for 15 minutes before or after. Ask the time with What time is it?

It's five. It's five o'clock. It's half past three. It's quarter to nine. What time is it?

Saying the time

TimeHow to say it
3:00It's three o'clock.
3:15It's quarter past three.
3:30It's half past three.
3:45It's quarter to four.

We can say "It's three" or "It's three o'clock" for an exact hour — both are correct.

Reading practice

Mia checks her watch. "What time is it?" she asks her brother. "It's quarter past eight," he says. "Our bus leaves at eight thirty!" They hurry to the bus stop. By the time they arrive, it's half past eight, and the bus is just pulling away. At school, the first class starts at nine o'clock. During lunch, Mia looks at the clock again. "It's quarter to twelve," she tells her friend. "We have fifteen more minutes." After school, Mia's mother picks her up. "It's five o'clock already," her mother says. "Time flies!"

5. Mia's mother picks her up at four o'clock.

Unit 13.1

The present simple

Use the present simple for habits and routines. Add -s to the verb for he, she, and it. Do not add -s for I, you, we, and they.

I eat lunch at noon every day. She eats lunch at 2pm every day. We walk to school together. He walks to work every morning. They drink coffee every morning.

Verb form in the present simple

SubjectVerb formExample
I / You / We / Theybase formeat, walk, drink
He / She / Itbase + -seats, walks, drinks

Some verbs need -es instead of just -s, like goes, watches, and does.

Grammar practice: choose the correct verb form

  1. (I / eat) I breakfast at seven.
  2. (she / eat) She lunch at noon.
  3. (he / walk) He to school every day.
  4. (they / drink) They tea in the morning.
  5. (it / open) The store at nine.
  6. (we / watch) We a movie every Friday.
  7. (he / go) My brother to the gym twice a week.
  8. (she / finish) She work at five.

On weekends, they relax at home and watch movies together.

2. Zoe drinks coffee every morning.

3. Zoe bakes bread before the bakery opens.

4. Noah starts work at eight.

5. Noah's father cooks dinner alone every night.

Unit 14.2

Prepositions and days of the week

Use on before a specific day of the week, like on Monday. Use from and to together to show a range of days, like from Monday to Friday.

I work on Mondays. I work from Monday to Friday. The store is closed on Sundays. We meet from Tuesday to Thursday. Classes start on Wednesday.

on vs. from...to

PrepositionUseExample
onone specific dayon Monday
from...toa range of daysfrom Monday to Friday

We can say "on Saturdays" (plural, a general habit) or "on Saturday" (this specific day) — both are common.

The days of the week

DayType
MondayWeekday
TuesdayWeekday
WednesdayWeekday
ThursdayWeekday
FridayWeekday
SaturdayWeekend
SundayWeekend

Days of the week always start with a capital letter, even in the middle of a sentence: "I have class on Tuesday and Thursday."

Grammar practice: choose the right preposition

  1. one day I work Mondays.
  2. a range of days, the start The gym is open Monday to Saturday.
  3. a range of days, the end The gym is open from Monday Saturday.
  4. one day The store is closed Sundays.
  5. a range of days Classes meet Tuesday to Thursday.
  6. one day We have a test Friday.
  7. a range of days The office is open Monday to Friday.
  8. one day Her birthday party is Saturday.

Reading practice

Every week, Priya follows the same schedule. She works from Monday to Friday at a small clinic. On Mondays, she starts early because the clinic is busy. From Tuesday to Thursday, she sees patients in the afternoon. On Fridays, she leaves work early to pick up her daughter. The clinic is closed on Sundays, so Priya spends the day with her family. On Saturdays, she usually relaxes at home or goes for a walk.

Check your understanding

1. Priya works from Monday to Friday.

2. The clinic is busiest on Fridays.

3. Priya sees patients in the afternoon from Tuesday to Thursday.

4. The clinic is open on Sundays.

5. Priya relaxes on Saturdays.

Units 14.8, 14.9

Frequency phrases

Use frequency phrases like once a week, twice a month, or three times a year to say how often something happens. Use every day, every week, or every month for something that happens each time.

I go to the gym twice a week. She visits her parents once a month. We travel three times a year. He calls his brother every day. They clean the house every week.

Saying how often

How oftenPhraseExample
1 timeonce a ___once a week
2 timestwice a ___twice a month
3+ times___ times a ___three times a year
each timeevery ___every day

After two, we say "twice," not "two times," although "two times" is also understood.

  6. happens each week The team practices week.
  7. two times a week I clean my apartment a week.
  8. three times a month She goes to yoga class a month.
