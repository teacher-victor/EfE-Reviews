Units 15.1, 15.3, 15.7

Negatives with "to be"

Add not after am, is, or are to make a negative sentence. For you, we, and they, we can contract to 're not or aren't. For he, she, and it, we can contract to 's not or isn't. There is no short contraction for "am not" — we only say I'm not.

I am a farmer. I am not a doctor. You're not a doctor. You aren't a doctor. He isn't from Spain. They aren't ready.

Negative forms of to be

SubjectNegativeExample
Iam notI'm not a doctor.
You / We / They're not / aren'tYou aren't a doctor.
He / She / It's not / isn'tHe isn't a teacher.

"I am not" has no short contracted negative form — we contract to "I'm not," never "I amn't."

  1. (I) I am a farmer.
  2. (you) You a doctor.
  3. (he) He from Spain.
  4. (she) She's a teacher.
  5. (we) We at home.
  6. (they) They ready yet.
  7. (it) It raining today.
  8. no contraction exists for this word I am tired.

Units 16.1, 16.3, 16.6

Present simple negative

Use do not (don't) for I, you, we, and they. Use does not (doesn't) for he, she, and it. The main verb always stays in the base form after don't or doesn't.

He does not work inside. He works outside. I work outside. I do not work inside. She doesn't like coffee. They don't live here.

Present simple negative

SubjectNegativeExample
I / You / We / Theydo not / don't + base verbI don't work inside.
He / She / Itdoes not / doesn't + base verbHe doesn't work inside.

After doesn't, the verb goes back to its base form: "He doesn't work," not "He doesn't works."

  5. (we) We eat meat.
  6. (it) It rain much here.
  7. the verb stays in the base form after doesn't He doesn't inside.
  8. (you) You need a ticket.

Units 17.1, 17.8

Simple questions

To ask a yes/no question with to be, put am, is, or are before the subject. To ask a yes/no question with another verb, use Do (I/you/we/they) or Does (he/she/it) before the subject, followed by the base verb.

Are you Canadian? Do you work in an office? Does she work in a school? Is he a doctor? Do they live nearby?

Forming a yes/no question

Question typeWord orderExample
to be questionAm/Is/Are + subjectAre you tired?
other verb, I/you/we/theyDo + subject + base verbDo you work here?
other verb, he/she/itDoes + subject + base verbDoes she work here?

After Does, the verb stays in its base form: "Does she work?" not "Does she works?"

  3. (she / work) she work in a school?
  4. a to be question, (he) he a doctor?
  5. (they / live) they live nearby?
  6. (it / rain) it rain a lot here?
  7. keep the verb in base form after Does Does he tea?
  8. a to be question, (we) we late?

Units 18.1, 18.2

Short answers

Answer a yes/no question with a short answer: a subject pronoun plus am/is/are or do/does (and not, if negative). A short answer never repeats the whole verb phrase.

Are you a doctor? Yes, I am. Do you work in an office? No, I don't. Is she a teacher? Yes, she is. Does he like coffee? No, he doesn't.

Short answers

QuestionShort yesShort no
Are you...?Yes, I am.No, I'm not.
Do you...?Yes, I do.No, I don't.
Is she...?Yes, she is.No, she isn't.
Does he...?Yes, he does.No, he doesn't.

A short answer never repeats the main verb: we say "Yes, I do," not "Yes, I do work."

Units 19.1, 19.2

Open questions with "to be"

Use a question word (What, Where, Who, When, Why, How) plus am, is, or are, plus the subject, to ask for information rather than a simple yes or no. The question word always comes first.

My name is Sarah. What is your name? Where are you from? Who is that? How old is she?

Question words

Question wordAsks aboutExample
Whata thingWhat is your name?
Wherea placeWhere are you from?
Whoa personWho is that?
Howa way or conditionHow are you?

The question word always comes first, before the form of to be.

Grammar practice: choose the right question word

  1. asking for a name is your name?
  2. asking for a place are you from?
  3. asking about a person is that woman?
  4. asking about condition are you today?
  5. asking for a reason is she upset?
  6. asking for a time is the meeting?
  7. asking about a person is your teacher?
  8. asking for a place is the nearest station?

Reading practice

She turns to a quiet girl. "How are you today?" she asks. "I'm a little nervous," the girl says. "Who is your cabin leader?" the counselor asks another camper. "I don't know yet," he says. "That's okay," says the counselor. "When is the welcome meeting? It's in ten minutes, so let's go find out together."

Check your understanding

2. The quiet girl feels very confident.

3. The camper knows who his cabin leader is.

4. The counselor asks "When is the welcome meeting?"

5. The welcome meeting is tomorrow.

Units 19.8, 19.9

Open questions using "do" and "does"

Use a question word plus do (I/you/we/they) or does (he/she/it), plus the subject, plus the base verb, to ask for information about an action.

When do you eat lunch? When does she eat lunch? Where do you work? What does he do on weekends?

Open questions with do / does

SubjectWord orderExample
I / You / We / Theyquestion word + do + subject + base verbWhere do you live?
He / She / Itquestion word + does + subject + base verbWhere does he live?

The main verb after do or does always stays in the base form: "What does she do?" not "What does she does?"

Grammar practice: choose do or does

  1. (you / eat) When you eat lunch?
  2. (she / eat) When she eat lunch?
  3. (you / work) Where you work?
  4. (he / do on weekends) What he do on weekends?
  5. (it / rain) How often it rain here?
  6. keep the verb in base form after does What does she on weekends?
  7. (they / study) Where they study?
  8. (he / finish work) When he finish work?

Reading practice

"One more question — when do you take your lunch break?" Noor asks. "Around noon," he says, smiling. "You ask a lot of questions!"
