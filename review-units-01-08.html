Review: Units 01–08

Unit 1.1

Introducing yourself

Use I am (short form I'm) and your name to introduce yourself. My name is works the same way. To introduce another person, say This is for one person, or These are for more than one. You can add His name is or Her name is too.

Hi, I'm Diego. My name is Priya. This is my friend, Noah. Her name is Ms. Alvarez. She is our teacher.

We often use the short form in speech, like "I'm Marco." The full form, like "I am Marco," sounds a little more formal.

  5. Introducing your cousins These are my cousins. Mia and Zoe.
  6. Checking someone's name Ms. Alvarez, right? Nice to meet you.
  7. Introducing yourself and a friend Nice to meet you! Aiko and Kenji.
  8. Introducing your teacher This is our teacher. Mrs. Owens.

Unit 3.1

How old are you?

Say your age with a subject, a form of to be, a number, and years old. Ask about someone's age with How old is/are...? We often drop "years old" in speech. "I'm 29" is the same as "I'm 29 years old."

I'm 27 years old. How old is your grandfather? We're both 19. They're turning 40 next month.

Subject + to be + years old

Subjectto beExample
IamI'm 29 years old.
YouareYou're 22 years old.
He / She / ItisShe's 8 years old.
We / TheyareThey're 70 years old.

In questions, "is" or "are" comes before the subject. We say "How old is she?" We do not say "How old she is?"

  5. Talking about a new kitten only 2 months old.
  6. You and a friend are the same age. both 22.
  7. Sara's little sister only 8 years old.
  8. Your new boss looks young. only 45? You look young!

Unit 5.1

Possessive adjectives

A possessive adjective comes before a noun. It shows who something belongs to: my, your, his, her, its, our, their. These words do not change. We use her for both "her book" and "her books."

Max is my dog. Is that your bike? Nina lost her keys. The company changed its logo.

Subject pronoun → possessive adjective

SubjectPossessive adjectiveExample
Imymy dog
Youyouryour bike
Hehishis book
Sheherher keys
Ititsits tail
Weourour house
Theytheirtheir toys

Do not confuse its (possessive, no apostrophe) with it's ("it is"). Use whose to ask about possession: "Whose keys are these?" "They're Nina's."

  5. (it) The company changed website last month.
  6. (he) Omar can't find glasses.
  7. (we) flight leaves at noon.
  8. Asking about ownership backpack is this?

Units 6.1, 6.5

Apostrophe with 's

Add 's to a singular noun to show possession. If a plural noun already ends in -s, add only an apostrophe. Some plural words, like children and men, do not end in -s. We still add 's to these words. For a name that ends in -s, like James, we can write James's or James'. When two people own one thing together, only the second name gets 's: Tom and Rosa's car.

Ana's brother is a doctor. My parents' car is red. The children's shoes are new. James's office is on the third floor.

Forming the possessive

Noun typeRuleExample
Singular nounadd 'sAna's brother
Plural noun ending in -sadd ' onlymy parents' car
Irregular plural (no -s)add 'sthe children's shoes
Singular name ending in -sadd 's (or ')James's office

Units 5.6, 8.1

This, that, these, those

This and that point to one thing. These and those point to more than one. This and these are for things close to you. That and those are for things far away. These words can also stand alone, without a noun after them: "I love this!"

This is my phone. That is your phone. These are my keys, and those are yours. I love this song.

These words work with or without a noun: "This bag is mine" and "This is mine" are both correct.

Unit 8.7

Determiners and pronouns

A possessive adjective comes before a noun, like my keys. A possessive pronoun stands alone and replaces the noun, like they're mine. Possessive pronouns never have an apostrophe. We write hers, yours, theirs, ours. We do not write her's or your's.

These are my shoes. Those shoes are mine. Is this your umbrella? Yes, it's yours. That desk is hers, not his.

ourours

His is the same word in both columns: "his keys" and "they're his."

Reading practice

At the airport, people look for their bags. "Is this yours?" a man asks a woman, holding up a green suitcase. "No, that's not mine," she says. "Mine has a yellow ribbon on it." Nearby, two brothers, Leo and Sam, both grab the same backpack. "That's mine!" says Leo. "No, I think it's his," says Sam, pointing at a stranger. The stranger checks his own bag and laughs. "You're right, this is mine." A family finds four red suitcases. "These are ours," says the father, counting them again. Leo looks at his empty hands. "I think this backpack really was mine after all."

2. The green suitcase belongs to the woman.

3. The backpack belongs to the stranger.

4. The family has four matching suitcases.

5. Sam keeps the backpack.
