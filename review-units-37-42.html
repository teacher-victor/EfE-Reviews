Review: Units 37–42

Units 37.1, 37.7

"Go" with gerunds, "play" with nouns

Use go plus a gerund (verb + -ing) for many activities, especially ones done alone, like "go swimming" or "go running." Use play plus a noun for sports and games with defined rules, like "play tennis" or "play chess."

I go swimming on Mondays. I play tennis with my brother on Fridays. She goes running every morning. They play soccer on weekends.

go + gerund vs. play + noun

VerbFollowed byExample
gogerund (-ing)go swimming
playnounplay tennis

Some activities always use "go": go shopping, go fishing, go camping. Team sports and games usually use "play": play soccer, play cards.

Grammar practice: choose go or play (in the right form)

  1. an activity done alone I swimming every weekend.
  2. a sport with rules He tennis on Saturdays.
  3. an activity done alone She running every morning.
  4. a game They chess after school.
  5. an activity done alone We hiking in the mountains.
  6. a sport with rules I basketball with my friends.
  7. an activity done alone He shopping every Friday.
  8. a game We cards on rainy days.

Unit 39.1

Adverbs of frequency

Adverbs of frequency (always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never) tell how often something happens. They usually go before the main verb, but after "to be."

I always watch TV at night. I sometimes go to the movies. She is always late. He never eats breakfast.

Adverbs of frequency

AdverbFrequencyExample
always100%I always watch TV.
usually / oftenmost of the timeI often watch TV.
sometimessome of the timeI sometimes watch TV.
rarelya little of the timeI rarely watch TV.
never0%I never watch TV.

Adverbs of frequency go before the main verb, but after the verb "to be": "She is always late," not "She always is late."

Grammar practice: choose the right adverb

  1. 100% of the time I watch TV at night.
  2. 0% of the time He eats vegetables.
  3. some of the time I go to the movies.
  4. after "to be" She is late.
  5. most of the time We walk to school.
  6. before the main verb (often) I coffee.
  7. a little of the time They eat fast food.
  8. 100% of the time, after to be He is on time.

Unit 39.6

Questions about free time

Use How often to ask about frequency. Use other question words like When or What with do/does to ask about free-time activities.

How often do you go on vacation? When does she go running? What do you do on weekends? How often do they play soccer?

Question words for free time

Question wordAsks aboutExample
How oftenfrequencyHow often do you exercise?
WhentimeWhen does she go running?
WhatactivityWhat do you do on weekends?

"How often" asks for a frequency answer, like "twice a week" or "every day," not a specific time.

Grammar practice: choose the right question word

  1. asking about frequency do you go on vacation?
  2. asking about time does she go running?
  3. asking about activity do you do on weekends?
  4. asking about frequency do they play soccer?
  5. asking about time do you usually relax?
  6. asking about frequency does he go to the gym?
  7. word order How often you exercise?
  8. word order, (she) When she go running?

Units 40.1, 40.7

Likes and dislikes

Use like, love, hate, and don't like to talk about likes and dislikes. Follow these verbs with a noun or a gerund (verb + -ing).

She likes tennis. Max doesn't like pizza. I love swimming. She hates shopping.

Verbs for likes and dislikes

VerbStrengthExample
lovestrong positiveI love swimming.
likepositiveShe likes tennis.
don't likenegativeMax doesn't like pizza.
hatestrong negativeShe hates shopping.

After these verbs, we can use a noun (like tennis) or a gerund (like swimming), but not the base verb alone.

Grammar practice: complete each sentence

  1. strong positive I swimming.
  2. negative, (Max) Max pizza.
  3. positive, (she) She tennis.
  4. strong negative, (she) She shopping.
  5. positive, gerund We playing video games.
  6. strong positive, (he) He dancing.
  7. negative, (I) I spicy food.
  8. strong negative, (they) They waiting in long lines.

Units 40.12, 40.13

Questions about likes and dislikes

Use Do you like...? to ask about likes with a yes/no answer. Use Why do you like...? to ask for a reason.

Do you like chocolate? Why do you like basketball? Does she like coffee? Why does he like hiking?

Asking about likes

Question typeFormExample
Yes/noDo/Does + subject + likeDo you like chocolate?
ReasonWhy + do/does + subject + likeWhy do you like basketball?

Answer a reason question with "because": "Why do you like basketball?" "Because it's exciting."

Grammar practice: complete each question

  1. yes/no question you like chocolate?
  2. asking for a reason do you like basketball?
  3. yes/no question, (she) she like coffee?
  4. asking for a reason, (he) does he like hiking?
  5. yes/no question, (they) they like spicy food?
  6. word order Why you like basketball?
  7. word order, (she) Why she like coffee?
  8. yes/no question, (it) it like cold weather?

Unit 42.1

Using "favorite"

Use favorite before a noun to say which thing you like most in a category. We often say favorite type of before a general category.

My favorite type of music is rock. My favorite color is blue. Pizza is my favorite food. What's your favorite movie?

Using favorite

StructureExample
favorite + nounmy favorite color
favorite type/kind of + categorymy favorite type of music

"Favorite" already means "most liked," so we don't say "most favorite" — that repeats the same idea.

Grammar practice: complete each sentence

  1. category with "type of" My type of music is rock.
  2. simple noun My color is blue.
  3. asking about a favorite What's your movie?
  4. simple noun Pizza is my food.
  5. category with "kind of" Her kind of book is mystery.
  6. avoid repeating the idea This is my song.
  7. simple noun His sport is basketball.
  8. category with "type of" Our type of weather is sunny.
